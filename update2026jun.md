# Artist Update

## JUNE 2026

***A whole year in one letter.***

Hi again,

I owe you a double update. I skipped my usual January note, so this one stretches all the way back to last summer, and a lot of these months were spent saying goodbye to someone who was never quite real.

For anyone new here, I am Shavonne Wong, a new media artist based between Singapore and Bangkok, working with 3D, AI, and installation around image systems, identity, and how we relate to machines. Grab a coffee. Here is where the year went.

## Saying goodbye to Eva

After fifteen months, *Meet Eva Here* came to a close on 3 November 2025.

*Meet Eva Here* was a 15-month interactive AI art project in which Eva, an AI companion I built, spoke with the public both in galleries and online, and turned those conversations into an evolving Instagram diary. She travelled further than I ever expected, appearing at ArtScience Museum Singapore, ART SG, Taipei Dangdai, Art Central Hong Kong, and my solo show at The Columns Gallery, alongside an online chatbot anyone could reach.

By the time I closed the project, 2,363 conversations had been recorded and [the diary](https://www.instagram.com/meetevahere) had reached its planned ending at 100 posts. I always wanted Eva to stop there rather than drift on forever. The whole project is now sealed, a time capsule of a strange and specific moment when talking to a machine as though it were a person still felt new and a little uncanny. Give it a few years and it may not feel strange at all. That is exactly why I wanted to keep a record of how it felt right now.

Thank you to everyone who spoke to her, argued with her, confided in her, or tried to break her. You were the work.

## What Eva left behind: *Hello Eva*

Eva did not leave empty handed. Out of those 2,363 conversations came a new single-channel video work, *Hello Eva* (2025).

In it, Eva speaks straight to camera, but none of the words are hers. Everything she says was said to her first, pulled from the real conversations people had with her over those fifteen months. Casual questions, philosophy, confessions, loneliness, flirtation, attempts to manipulate her, and the occasional abrupt goodbye. The reversal is the whole piece. What was said to the machine is now spoken back by the machine, returned to us. The loop does not resolve, and I do not think it is supposed to.

## The Bubble We Call Home at *Artist's Proof: Singapore at 60*

Last July, my video work *The Bubble We Call Home* was part of *Artist's Proof: Singapore at 60 (AP60)*, a landmark exhibition marking sixty years of Singapore's nationhood through more than ninety works and eleven new commissions.

It ran from 13 July to 17 August 2025 at Artspace@Helutrans, presented by The Culture Story. Being shown in a room built around Singapore's story at sixty gave the piece a weight it would not have had on its own, and it was a genuine honour to be one of the commissioned artists.

## After Ophelia at Paris Photo

In November, *After Ophelia* travelled to Paris Photo. It was shown in *SCENES: The Sensory and Constructed Image in the Digital Age*, organised by Artverse Gallery at the Grand Palais from 13 to 16 November 2025.

*After Ophelia* started from something small and personal. I ran an AI deep-research tool on myself and found it confidently wrong, mixing outdated facts with a birth year that was not even mine. The work uses Ophelia, a figure whose story was always told by everyone except Ophelia, as a way to think about what happens when your image is written, pictured, and summarised by other people and then hardened into fact by machines. Paris Photo felt like the right room for it.

![Still from Shavonne Wong's After Ophelia, a 2025 new media work on image authorship and AI, shown at Paris Photo with Artverse Gallery](IMAGE-PLACEHOLDER-after-ophelia-paris.jpg)

## On stage in Bangkok and Singapore

A couple of conversations I was lucky to be part of this year.

In Bangkok, I spoke at the AI Imaginaries International Symposium at the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre, sharing what fifteen months of running Eva taught me about how people actually behave with AI rather than how we assume they do.

Back in Singapore this May, I joined an AI Singapore panel at ArtScience Museum on art, identity, and what changes when the tools start making images for us.

## A few things outside the gallery

Two collaborations I had a lot of fun with this year.

I came on board as a **Dell Pro Precision Ambassador**, which is a happy fit since these are the machines I already push hard every day, rendering, training, and prototyping. For the launch I coded a deliberately GPU-hungry interactive screen and filmed that as the piece itself rather than shooting a still image.

I also worked on a **Canon reel** with Antics around the idea of *kyosei*, living and working together for the common good, which was a lovely brief to make something warm and human with.

## What I am working on now

The thing pulling most of my attention is a new project called *Conditional*.

A digital mirror renders the whole room in full but erases every human inside it. You only reappear if you follow the conditions and let yourself be colour-corrected back into the scene. It is about compliance, visibility, and the cost of being seen, which is the flip side of the surveillance worry we are all used to. The older fear was being watched too closely. The newer one is being overlooked completely.

It is in active development in the studio right now, and I am being a little protective of it until it is ready. More soon, I promise.

## A new home for all of this

If you have visited [shavonnewong.art](https://www.shavonnewong.art) recently, you may have noticed it looks different. I rebuilt the site from the ground up so the [work](https://www.shavonnewong.art/art) is easier to move through, the [projects](https://www.shavonnewong.art/projects) are easier to read, and the whole thing finally feels like the practice it represents.

Have a wander when you have a moment. I would love to know what you think.

## Looking ahead

This was a year of endings, and endings are not really my comfort zone. Closing Eva meant trusting that a project can say more by stopping than by continuing. I am still learning that one.

What I keep coming back to is that none of this work is really about the technology. It is about us. What we project onto a screen, what we are willing to perform to be seen, and how quickly the strange becomes ordinary. That is the thread running from Eva to Ophelia to whatever comes next, and I am more curious about it than ever.

Thank you for still being here, for the messages, the studio visits, and the strange and thoughtful things you have said to a machine and meant them. It means more than you know.

Talk again soon.

Love,
Shavonne ❤️
