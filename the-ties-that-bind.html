Overview

The Ties That Bind grew from a belief that technology can heighten storytelling without becoming the spectacle itself. Each artwork is built around a virtual model and a human condition: identity, time, perception, suspended life, and shared burden.

Every piece had a physical exhibition life and a digital artwork life. In the room, viewers activated the work through sensors, augmented reality, or real-time systems. On-chain, the related works carried dynamic logic, making collection, attention, or network activity part of how the image changed.

The project followed from earlier experiments with dynamic works, especially the discovery that a digital artwork could remain alive after release. When a two-week exhibition space became available in March 2023, the question became how far that responsiveness could be pushed between a gallery, a body, and a blockchain system.

The result was a suite of works that existed twice: as physical encounters using screens, prints, AR, and custom footfall sensing, and as digital works whose behaviors were shaped by Ethereum activity, the viewer's attention, or the act of collecting. The project treats interaction as a way to make emotional states legible.

Works

Six 1/1 works and one editioned work, across five human conditions. Each pairs a virtual model with a feeling and a system, identity, time, perception, suspension, and shared burden, and each lived twice: once in the gallery room, once on-chain.

Introduction

Duality of Self looks at the contradictory, co-existing parts of personality: the inward self and the outward self. The work turns that tension into two responsive states, making identity feel less like a fixed portrait and more like a living negotiation between solitude and social presence.

Physical exhibition version

The gallery version used a custom footfall sensor system developed with Serial Co. As the number of people in the room changed, the installation switched between introverted and extroverted states, allowing the work to respond to the social energy around it.

NFT version

The blockchain version reframed that same duality through Ethereum traffic. Instead of visitors in a room, network activity determined which state appeared, making the chain itself an active participant in the artwork's expression of self.

II

Time, the march we can’t feel

Introduction

The Invisible March of Time is about the way time moves even when it feels still. The daily changes in a face are almost impossible to see, yet they accumulate into years. The work asks viewers to sit with mortality, change, and the urgency of being present with the people they love.

Physical exhibition version

In the gallery, two independent figures aged over thirty minutes. The transformation was deliberately gradual: viewers could stay and watch the subtle movement, or return later and encounter the accumulated difference.

NFT version

On-chain, the aging logic became personal and cyclical. The figures age in relation to the artist's own age, and when the cycle reaches one hundred, the artwork fades and begins again, echoing both impermanence and renewal.

III

Perception, looking as an act

A still image that only opens when the viewer leans in, interiority made visible by attention itself.

05.

Beyond the Surface

2023Augmented reality image · interactive digital artwork · 1/1
1/1 · sold

Beyond the Surface begins as a still image and opens through interaction. In the exhibition, an Instagram AR filter transformed the printed figure into a moving mirror, making hidden interiority visible only when the viewer came closer. The digital version carried that reveal into an interactive NFT, where looking became an action rather than a passive state.

For acquisitions: studio@shavonnewong.art

SuperRare record →Etherscan →Ethereum

Introduction

Beyond the Surface challenges the instinct to judge from the outside. It begins with a quiet seated figure and asks viewers to move closer, using interaction to reveal that what first appears still or simple has a deeper interior life.

Physical exhibition version

In the room, the work was presented as a print with an Instagram AR filter. When viewers engaged with the QR code and camera view, the still portrait opened into a moving reflection with rippling water, flowers, and cool blue atmosphere.

NFT version

The digital version kept the same invitation to look deeper. On desktop, interaction unlocked the animated state; on mobile, touching the figure's eyes triggered the reveal. The act of looking became the mechanism that changed the work.

IV

Suspension, held while life moves

A solitary figure paused as the light keeps changing around her, the room’s presence setting the pace.

In Suspension

2023Interactive installation · dynamic digital artwork · 1/1

In Suspension holds a solitary figure in stillness while light changes around her. In the gallery, visitor presence affected the speed of those light changes, making time feel responsive without releasing the figure from her pause. The work is quiet, but it carries the project's central concern: what it means to be held in place while life continues to move.

For acquisitions: studio@shavonnewong.art

SuperRare →Etherscan →Ethereum

Introduction

In Suspension comes from the feeling of being paused while life continues around you. The seated figure is quiet and almost still, but the changing light around her suggests time passing, pressure shifting, and the strange dislocation that can arrive during difficult seasons.

Physical exhibition version

The installation used footfall sensing to let visitors influence the speed of the light transitions. People in the room could sit with the figure, and their presence affected the pace of the environment without breaking the work's stillness.

NFT version

The NFT version extends the same idea into attention itself. The longer a viewer remains with the work, the more its state can evolve, making quiet engagement part of the artwork's emotional and technical structure.

Introduction

The Weight of the World is about the burdens people carry and the relief that can come through community. The figure begins with a heavy load, turning emotional responsibility into a visible weight.

Physical exhibition version

Process

The project used footfall sensors, AR, dynamic display systems, and blockchain-linked logic. The technical systems were intentionally subordinated to emotional behavior: how a room changes a person, how time quietly accumulates, how attention opens an image, and how support can alter the weight someone carries.

The works were shown through a mix of physical prints, portrait-format displays, wide screens, QR-enabled labels, and sensor-linked systems. This range of presentation formats mattered: each artwork needed a different relationship between visitor, body, and interface.

Artist statement

The Ties That Bind treats technology as a narrative material, not as spectacle. Each work uses a technical structure to make a human condition visible: dual selves, aging, hidden depth, suspended time, and communal responsibility. The exhibition asks viewers to notice how their own presence changes the story.

Supporting texts

The project extends Wong's earlier experiments with dynamic artwork and asks how digital systems can carry intimacy without reducing the work to novelty. Its supporting ideas come from installation art, responsive media, performance, and the social language of blockchain collecting.
