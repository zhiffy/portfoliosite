The Ties That Bind.

This project is a blend of my love for storytelling, virtual models, physical installation, sensors, augmented reality, and blockchain dynamics across six 1/1 works and one editioned work.

I wanted presence, attention, and collection to become part of the story.

Overview

The Ties That Bind grew from my belief that technology can enhance storytelling without becoming the spectacle itself. I built each artwork around a virtual model and a human condition: identity, time, perception, suspended life, and shared burden.

Each piece existed in two versions: one that lived in the physical exhibition, and one that was born in the digital realm of the blockchain. In the room, I experimented with footfall sensors, augmented reality, responsive displays, and real-time systems so visitors could experience the works in a more active way.

The idea followed from my earlier experiments with dynamic artworks, especially the moment I realized a digital artwork could stay alive after release. When I was offered a two-week exhibition venue in March 2023, I wanted to push that further between a gallery, a body, and a blockchain system.

Creating this project was both fun and challenging. It gave me space to explore a fusion of physical and digital art, where the same work could be encountered in the room and on-chain, and where interaction could make emotional states more visible.

Works

I made six 1/1 works and one editioned work, each built around a virtual model, a human condition, and a technical system: identity, time, perception, suspension, and shared burden. Every piece lived twice, once in the gallery room and once on-chain.

Introduction

Duality of Self came from my fascination with the contradictory sides of personality: the inward self and the outward self. I wanted the work to feel less like a fixed portrait and more like a living negotiation between solitude and social presence.

Physical exhibition version

For the gallery version, I worked with Serial Co on a custom footfall sensor system. As the number of people in the room changed, the installation switched between introverted and extroverted states, so visitors became part of the artwork's behavior.

NFT version

For the blockchain version, I reframed that duality through Ethereum traffic. Instead of visitors in a room, network activity determined which state appeared, making the chain itself an active participant in the artwork.

Time, the march we can’t feel

I wanted to make time visible in the way we usually experience it: almost impossible to notice day by day, but undeniable when we return later.

Introduction

The Invisible March of Time came from thinking about how time keeps moving even when it feels still. The daily changes in a face are almost impossible to see, yet they accumulate into years. I wanted the work to remind viewers of mortality, change, and the urgency of being present with the people they love.

Physical exhibition version

In the gallery, I presented two independent figures aging over thirty minutes. The transformation was deliberately gradual: viewers could stay and watch the subtle movement, or return later and encounter the accumulated difference.

NFT version

On-chain, I made the aging logic personal and cyclical. The figures age in relation to my own age, and when the cycle reaches one hundred, I let the artwork fade and begin again, echoing impermanence and renewal.

III

Perception, looking as an act

I wanted this work to challenge how quickly we judge from the outside, and to reward the viewer only when they choose to look deeper.

I made Beyond the Surface begin as a still image and open through interaction. In the exhibition, an Instagram AR filter transformed the printed figure into a moving mirror, revealing hidden layers only when viewers came closer. For the digital version, I carried that same reveal into an interactive NFT, where looking became an action rather than a passive state.

SuperRare record →Etherscan →Ethereum

Introduction

Beyond the Surface came from wanting to challenge the instinct to judge from the outside. I started with a quiet seated figure and used interaction to reveal that what first appears still or simple has a deeper interior life.

Physical exhibition version

In the room, I presented the work as a print with an Instagram AR filter. When viewers engaged with the QR code and camera view, the still portrait opened into a moving reflection with rippling water, flowers, and a cool blue atmosphere.

NFT version

For the digital version, I kept the same invitation to look deeper. On desktop, interaction unlocked the animated state; on mobile, touching the figure's eyes triggered the reveal. I wanted the act of looking to become the mechanism that changed the work.

IV

Suspension, held while life moves

I made this from the feeling of being suspended in time, especially during difficult seasons, while life keeps moving around you.

In Suspension holds a solitary figure in stillness while light changes around her. In the gallery, I used visitor presence to affect the speed of those light changes, making time feel responsive without releasing the figure from her pause. I wanted the work to feel quiet, but emotionally heavy: a moment of being held in place while life continues to move.

For acquisitions: studio@shavonnewong.art

SuperRare →Etherscan →Ethereum

Introduction

In Suspension came from my reflections on life, difficulty, and the moments where it feels like everything is passing by while you are stuck in one place. The seated figure is quiet and almost still, but the changing light around her suggests time passing and pressure shifting.

Physical exhibition version

For the installation, I used footfall sensing so visitors could influence the speed of the light transitions. People in the room could sit with the figure, and their presence affected the pace of the environment without breaking the work's stillness.

NFT version

For the NFT version, I extended the same idea into attention itself. The longer a viewer remains with the work, the more its state can evolve, making quiet engagement part of the artwork's emotional and technical structure.

Introduction

The Weight of the World is about the burdens we carry and the relief that can come through community. I wanted the figure's heavy load to make emotional responsibility visible.

The Ties That Bind · Solo exhibition, 2023

Process

I used footfall sensors, AR, dynamic display systems, and blockchain-linked logic, but I never wanted the technology to take center stage. I used it to support emotional behavior: how a room changes a person, how time quietly accumulates, how attention opens an image, and how support can alter the weight someone carries.

I showed the works through physical prints, portrait-format displays, wide screens, QR-enabled labels, and sensor-linked systems. This range of presentation formats mattered to me because each artwork needed a different relationship between visitor, body, and interface.

Artist statement

With The Ties That Bind, I treated technology as a narrative material, not as spectacle. I used technical structures to make human conditions visible: dual selves, aging, hidden depth, suspended time, and communal responsibility. I wanted viewers to notice how their own presence could change the story.

Supporting texts

This project extends my earlier experiments with dynamic artwork and asks how digital systems can carry intimacy without reducing the work to novelty. I was drawing from installation art, responsive media, performance, and the social language of blockchain collecting.
