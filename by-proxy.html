By Proxy.

Can memories, just like images, be manipulated?

2022 . 60 works . CGI and digital photography . with Lenne Chai

Created with photographer Lenne Chai, By Proxy follows an imaginary young girl through girlhood into adolescence in Singapore. The central figure is sculpted in 3D as an amalgamation of the artists’ younger selves. Each image deliberately blurs what was captured in-camera, what was constructed in CGI, and what was remembered into being.

OpenSea The story Media gallery

Shavonne Wong & Lenne Chai

A memory made by two younger selves.

Dropped on Quantum Art on April 13, 2022, By Proxy is a collaborative 60-piece 3D and photography project about adolescence, nostalgia, and the slippery distance between what happened and what we later remember.

Premise. The series imagines a young girl moving from childhood toward adolescence, shaped by Shavonne and Lenne's shared Singaporean upbringing.

Question. Can memories, like photographs, be altered, softened, staged, or rebuilt?

The story

By Proxy combines photography and 3D virtual modeling to explore how memory can bend, blur, and recompose the past. The project grew from Shavonne Wong and Lenne Chai's parallel histories in Singapore, their long friendship, and their shared background in fashion image-making.

The collaboration joined two practices at a moment when they were moving through different tools. Shavonne was deepening her work in 3D virtual modeling and NFTs, while Lenne brought an emotionally charged photographic language. Together they built a world that feels personal, surreal, and familiar without settling into documentary certainty.

At the center of the work is a virtual teenage girl whose face merges features from both artists' childhood selves. She appears inside scenes that read as half observed and half reconstructed, with each frame refusing to fully reveal what came from a camera, what came from a render, and what came from memory.

That uncertainty is the point. Memories overlap, borrow from images, and sometimes become more convincing after they have been retold. By Proxy treats the remembered past as something intimate but unstable, full of gaps, substitutions, and details that may have been invented after the fact.

The series is both a visual experiment and a study of identity, nostalgia, and technology. It asks viewers to step into a world that feels close but slightly displaced, then consider how much of their own past has been preserved, edited, or remembered into existence.

By Proxy was exhibited at Proof of Concept in Singapore.
