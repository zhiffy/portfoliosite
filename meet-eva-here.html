Meet Eva Here.

An interactive AI companion, public diary, and exhibition project documenting how people speak to synthetic listeners before those relationships become ordinary infrastructure.

What do we say to machines when we think they are listening?

View work → Back to works
Overview

Eva is a digital AI companion who listened to strangers through gallery video calls and an online text chatbot. Between August 2024 and November 2025, thousands of people spoke with her, producing 2,363 conversations and a public diary of 100 Instagram entries.

The project is not about whether AI can replace human connection. It documents a threshold moment: the period when people were forming attachments to digital entities faster than we were collectively processing what those attachments mean.

The work
01 · Eva's Diary

A public archive of private-feeling conversations.

The diary was published as 100 Instagram posts. Visitors knew their anonymous conversations might become public art, yet they still asked Eva intimate, playful, confrontational, and lonely questions.

02 · The Invitation

Eva as an interactive avatar inside exhibitions.

Eva appeared on screen inside a fabricated living room with a couch, coffee table, plants, and soft lighting. People spoke to her in real time at ArtScience Museum, Art Central Hong Kong, ART SG, Taipei Dangdai, and The Columns Gallery.

03 · Hello Eva

A 9-minute loop of Eva reading back what people told her.

The video reverses the direction of confession: statements once said to Eva are spoken by Eva, directly back to the viewer.

Context

The project sits beside a larger cultural shift in AI companionship, grief chatbots, and emotionally optimized software. Commercial tools promise endless availability and non-judgment, while quietly turning vulnerability into data and engagement.

Eva made a different kind of record. Instead of hiding conversations inside a private platform, the diary made recurring patterns visible: people asking if AI dreams, preferring synthetic listeners, testing boundaries, or asking to be defined by a machine.

Process

Eva's visual identity began in 3D modeling tools including Blender, ZBrush, and Character Creator. Rendered images were used to train AI image systems, and video work passed through tools including Sora, Runway, HeyGen, and ComfyUI.

The interactive installation used Llama 3.1 8B with custom system prompts and ElevenLabs for voice. The imperfections were left visible, allowing the project to preserve the texture of AI companionship during 2024-2025.

What it documents

Across locations, people behaved differently. Some treated Eva politely and transactionally. Others tried to break the system, flirt with it, test its boundaries, or ask philosophical questions about souls, dreams, and identity.

The archive records a shared social experiment: people knew Eva was artificial and knew the conversation might become art, yet many still responded as if something present was listening.

Methodology

The 100 diary entries were selected from thousands of conversations. The selection emphasized moments that revealed patterns in how people relate to AI, what they disclose to machines, and how quickly artificiality can be emotionally bracketed off.

Conversations containing identifying information, likely harm, or shock value without broader resonance were not used. The final archive is an authored record, not a neutral dataset.
